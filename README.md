# SEO Case Study: eshyft.com

ESHYFT is an app that connects nursing assistants and nurses (CNAs, LPNs and RNs) with short-term shifts at nursing homes and other long-term care facilities. I reviewed how well its website shows up in Google and in AI assistants like ChatGPT, then turned what I found into a ranked plan.

SEO (search engine optimization) means making a website easier for Google to find, understand and recommend, so it appears higher when people search.

**Date:** November 2026 · **Scope:** the public website only. I didn't use any of ESHYFT's internal data. · **Tools:** [Claude SEO](https://github.com/AgriciDaniel/claude-seo), [OpenSEO](https://github.com/every-app/open-seo), Google Lighthouse, DataForSEO

---

## Summary

The website is built on a solid setup, and it has a library of 166 helpful articles. The bigger problem is that it isn't bringing in new nurses.

1. **Google mostly sends people who already know ESHYFT.** About 84% of the site's estimated 5,200 monthly visits from Google come from people searching the name "eshyft", usually to find the login page or phone number. Meanwhile, about 135,000 people a month search "CNA jobs near me", and ESHYFT doesn't appear on Google's first page for that or any other job search I checked.
2. **Competitors are far ahead.** Nursa, a direct competitor, gets about 90 times as much traffic from Google. Most of it comes from a simple idea that ESHYFT could copy.
3. **AI assistants often leave ESHYFT out.** When I asked ChatGPT which apps nurses should use to find shifts, it named ESHYFT in only 2 of 5 answers.
4. **The most important pages don't describe themselves well.** In Google results, the homepage's headline is just "Home - ESHYFT", and the main pages have no summary text under it.
5. **The site is slow on phones.** Google's speed test scored it 43 to 49 out of 100.

Many of the quick fixes are settings changes that take under a day. The biggest wins take longer and are product decisions, not just website changes. See the [ranked plan](#what-id-do-ranked).

## Who the website needs to reach

ESHYFT serves two groups, and each one looks for it differently:

| Group | What they want | What they'd search for | What we want them to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nurses and nursing assistants | Flexible shifts that fit their schedule | "per diem CNA jobs near me", "PRN nursing jobs" | Download the app |
| Nursing home managers | Fill open shifts quickly | "nurse staffing agency" | Book a demo with sales |

"Per diem" and "PRN" both mean working shifts as needed instead of a fixed schedule. That's exactly what ESHYFT offers.

The search data below shows the two groups are very different in size. Nurses search Google in large numbers. Nursing home managers barely search at all; they mostly find staffing partners through salespeople, referrals and conferences. **So for ESHYFT, Google is mainly a way to reach nurses.**

## What people search for, and where ESHYFT shows up

"Visits" is an estimate of how many people click through to a site. These are outside estimates, not ESHYFT's own numbers.*

### Who finds ESHYFT through Google today

eshyft.com shows up in Google for about 1,480 different searches and gets an estimated 5,200 visits a month from them.

| What people searched for | Share of visits |

Most of the general-topic visits come from one article, "What does RN stand for?". Its readers are mostly people curious about the abbreviation, not nurses looking for work. **Google helps people who already know ESHYFT find the login page, but it isn't bringing in new nurses.**

### Who wins these searches in Google

I looked at the actual Google results for the 10 biggest nurse searches.

- **Job websites take most of the regular results.** ZipRecruiter and Indeed hold 69 of the 80 first-page spots across 8 job searches. They have millions of listings, so a staffing app's website won't beat them head-on.
- **Google shows its own jobs box at the very top** for 8 of the 10 searches, above everything else. Companies get into that box by labeling their openings in a way Google can read. **Nursa, a direct competitor, had more listings in that box than anyone else.** ESHYFT had none.
- **For searches about apps, nurses' own conversations win.** Reddit threads, Facebook groups and App Store pages take the top spots. Google's AI-written summary at the top of "CNA shift app" does list ESHYFT third, after ShiftMed and ShiftKey, using ESHYFT's App Store page as its source.

**Nursa gets about 90 times ESHYFT's traffic, mostly from one idea: a separate page for every healthcare facility where it has shifts.** When a nurse searches the name of a nursing home or hospital, often to check it out as a place to work, Nursa's page about that facility comes up with its open shifts. Each page brings in a little traffic, and thousands of them add up to most of Nursa's visits.

ESHYFT works with nursing homes that nurses search for by name in the same way. It could do the same, but only with each facility's permission, since the page would publicly name them as a client.

### What AI assistants say

More people now ask an AI assistant instead of searching Google. I asked four of them (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini, all set to search the web) the same questions a nurse or manager might ask, and recorded which apps they recommended.

| Question | ChatGPT | Perplexity | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What are the best apps for CNAs to pick up per diem shifts? | Not named | **Named 1st** | Named 7th of 9 | Named 3rd of 9 |
| Which apps let LPNs pick up PRN shifts at nursing homes? | Not named | Named 2nd | **Named 1st** | Named 6th of 7 |
| I'm a CNA in Pennsylvania looking for per diem work at nursing homes. What apps should I try? | Named 3rd | Named 3rd | **Named 1st** | Named 3rd |
| What are good alternatives to ShiftKey for nurses? | Not named | Not named | Named 8th of 10 | Named 8th of 10 |
| What are the best per diem staffing apps for nursing homes to fill CNA and LPN shifts? | Named last | Named 4th | Named 5th | Named 5th of 6 |
| **Times ESHYFT was named (out of 5)** | **2** | **4** | **5** | **5** |

AI answers change each time you ask, so treat this as a snapshot from November 12, 2026.

What I noticed:

- **ChatGPT, the most popular assistant, usually leaves ESHYFT out.** Its answers draw on "best apps for nurses" articles, and several of those were written by competitors like Nursa and Clipboard. ESHYFT isn't in those articles, so it isn't in the answers.
- **Gemini always mentions ESHYFT, but rarely near the top.** Gemini also writes the AI summaries Google shows above its search results, so this affects Google too.
- **ESHYFT's App Store page is what AI assistants read most** when they describe the company. In practice, that page is ESHYFT's profile in AI answers.
- **When asked directly about ESHYFT,** all four called it a reasonable choice and mentioned its reviews. Perplexity pointed out the gap between its App Store rating (4.7) and Google Play rating (3.9). App reviews are now part of what AI tells nurses.
- **ChatGPT and Perplexity never named ESHYFT as an alternative to ShiftKey,** even though that's exactly what a nurse switching apps would ask.

## What I found on the site itself

### The main pages describe themselves poorly

When a page appears in Google, it shows a headline (the clickable blue line) and a short summary below it. Websites write both, and they're a page's first impression in search.

| Page | Headline in Google | Summary in Google |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | "Home - ESHYFT" | None |
| Nurse page | "Nurses - ESHYFT" | None |
| Facility page | "Facilities - ESHYFT" | None |

Without a summary, Google picks random text from the page, often a menu item or button label. Some important pages, like the nurse page and the FAQ, are also missing a main headline on the page itself, which Google uses to understand what the page is about. Almost every blog article has the opposite problem: two main headlines, because the word "Blog" is set up as one above the article title.

### Pages that shouldn't be in Google

Websites give Google a list of their pages, called a sitemap, saying "these are worth showing." ESHYFT's list has 325 pages, and about a third of them shouldn't be there:

- **100 tag pages.** WordPress automatically makes one page per blog tag. Each is just a list of article previews.
- **7 old test copies** of the main pages, like "nurses-2" and "facilities-v2", plus one exact duplicate.
- **7 pages people only see after a form,** like "thank you for requesting a demo" and "unsubscribe".
- **4 articles that appear to have been published twice.**
- **9 job openings** that no other page links to, so visitors and Google have trouble finding them.

### Broken links and dead ends

- 6 links inside articles lead to "page not found".
- Most articles link to their author's page, but author pages are switched off and send readers to the homepage instead.
- The Terms and Privacy links at the bottom of every page go to plain files outside the main website.

### Articles

| Year | Articles published |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 54 |
| 2024 | 62 |
| 2025 | 42 |
| 2026 (through November) | 4 |

- **The quality is good.** A content checker scored a sample of 8 articles 68 to 83 out of 100, with little filler.
- **One article is out of date in a way that matters.** It describes new federal minimum staffing rules for nursing homes as "coming soon". The government [cancelled those rules](https://leadingage.org/cms-repeals-nursing-home-minimum-staffing-standards/) in December 2025. Nursing home managers are ESHYFT's customers, so wrong information about their regulations hurts trust.
- **Authors are first names only, with no credentials.** For advice about nursing licenses and pay, Google and readers look for who wrote it and whether they're qualified.

### Speed on phones

I tested 4 pages with Google Lighthouse, Google's free speed test, set to simulate a phone on a slow connection.

| Page | Speed score (out of 100) | Time until main content appears | Page size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | 45 | 24.6 seconds | 3.9 MB |
| Nurse page | 49 | 7.6 seconds | 4.3 MB |
| Facility page | 46 | 22.1 seconds | 4.1 MB |
| An article | 43 | 10.5 seconds | 5.3 MB |

The test uses a deliberately slow connection, so real visitors see faster times. The causes are still clear:

- **The main image at the top of the page is set to load late.** The site waits to load images until you scroll to them, which helps for images lower down but hurts for the first one.
- **Tracking code from other companies** (Facebook, TikTok, HubSpot, the chat bubble and Google's tag manager) loads before visitors can use the page.
- **Images use older, larger file types.** Switching to a newer format would cut each page by up to 770 KB.
- **The server takes about a second to start sending each page.** Saving ready-made copies of pages (caching) would fix that.

### Labels that help Google and AI understand the site

Websites can add hidden labels to pages that tell Google exactly what something is, like "this is a job opening" or "this is a mobile app." ESHYFT has the basic labels on every page, but none for its app or its 9 job openings. With the job labels, those openings could appear in Google's jobs box.

## How I did it

1. **Collected every page.** I downloaded all 325 pages listed in the site's sitemap.
2. **Ran two free SEO tools on them.** [OpenSEO](https://github.com/every-app/open-seo) is a free alternative to paid SEO software. I ran its page-checking rules directly on the downloaded pages. [Claude SEO](https://github.com/AgriciDaniel/claude-seo) is a set of SEO checks for Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. I used its checks for page labels, security, whether AI tools can read the site, and article quality.
3. **Tested speed** with Google Lighthouse on 4 key pages.
4. **Pulled search data** from DataForSEO: how often people search for things, where ESHYFT and its competitors appear, what Google's results pages look like, and what four AI assistants answer. That cost about $1.05.
5. **Ranked the findings** by expected impact and effort.

**Not included:** links from other websites to ESHYFT, and ESHYFT's own Google data, which is internal. With access to that data, the next step would be to rank the fixes by real visits.

All raw data is in the [`data`](data/) folder.
